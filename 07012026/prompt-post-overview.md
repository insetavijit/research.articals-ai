Below is a **more powerful, research-grade prompt**, still reusable, but designed to extract **deeper, structured observations** without drifting into summary or opinion.

---

### **Extended Text-Analysis Prompt (Observational Mode)**

> Analyze the following text and generate a **structured list of observations**.
> Focus exclusively on **how the text is written**, not on what it claims.
>
> Your observations should cover:
>
> 1. **Structural Design**
>
>    * Lead construction (fact-led, narrative, hook-based, etc.)
>    * Paragraph sequencing and information layering
>    * Use of background vs breaking information
> 2. **Style & Language**
>
>    * Sentence complexity and pacing
>    * Vocabulary register (technical, neutral, emotive, institutional)
>    * Use of qualifiers, hedging, or certainty markers
> 3. **Tone & Voice**
>
>    * Emotional distance or neutrality
>    * Presence or absence of judgment
>    * Institutional vs personal voice
> 4. **Authority & Credibility Signals**
>
>    * Use of named sources, experts, or data
>    * Attribution patterns (direct quotes vs reported speech)
>    * Reliance on third-party validation
> 5. **Framing & Emphasis**
>
>    * What is foregrounded vs backgrounded
>    * Repetition of themes or keywords
>    * Placement of geopolitical, economic, or social context
> 6. **Implicit Bias & Narrative Constraints**
>
>    * Subtle framing choices
>    * Risk-avoidance or compliance language
>    * What is notably omitted or deferred
>
> **Rules**:
>
> * Do not summarize the topic.
> * Do not fact-check or add external context.
> * Do not speculate on intent.
> * Keep each observation concise and analytical.
> * Output as a numbered or bulleted list only.
